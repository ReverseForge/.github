# 🤝 Code of Conduct – ReverseForge

**Last Updated:** May 10, 2026

## Our Pledge

As members, contributors, and leaders of **ReverseForge**, we pledge to make participation in our community a harassment-free experience for everyone. We are committed to acting with integrity, professionalism, and respect in all interactions—whether in public channels, private communications, or during coordinated vulnerability disclosure.

## Our Standards

### ✅ Expected Behavior
- **Assume good faith.** Security research is complex; misunderstandings happen. Start from a position of respect.
- **Be constructive.** Critique the vulnerability, not the person. Offer solutions, not just criticism.
- **Respect confidentiality.** Honor embargo periods, protect sensitive data, and never weaponize a disclosure.
- **Communicate clearly.** Prefer technical precision over vague statements. Be open to being wrong.
- **Collaborate ethically.** We hack software, not people. No doxxing, no harassment, no ego battles.

### ❌ Unacceptable Behavior
- Harassment, intimidation, or discrimination of any kind.
- Publishing others' private information (doxxing) without explicit consent.
- Threatening, extorting, or blackmailing vendors under the guise of vulnerability disclosure.
- Knowingly submitting false or unverified vulnerability reports.
- Any conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional security research setting.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be reported by contacting the team at:

📧 **`team@reverseforge.dev`**

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. We reserve the right to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned with this Code of Conduct.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all organization spaces (GitHub repositories, issue trackers, project boards, and associated communications) and also applies when an individual is officially representing ReverseForge in public spaces.
